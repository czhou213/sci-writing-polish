# Figures, Tables, and Legends

**Course framing, p. 3:** “Ensuring your data is presented effectively in graphs, tables, and images.” Day 1 Module 3 occupies PDF pp. 215–303. The checks below are editorial applications, not verbatim slide instructions.

Edit the supplied presentation. A polishing request does not authorize recalculating data or recreating every figure. If only a legend is provided, state that the visual cannot be checked.

## Presentation checks

Make the subject, comparison, and main pattern clear; ensure visual encodings match the data; reduce decoration without removing information needed to evaluate the evidence.

- For categorical quantities, bars or tables may be appropriate; assess the baseline when bar length encodes magnitude.
- For continuous measurements, mean-only bars can hide distributions. Suggest points, boxes, or violins as appropriate to sample size, overlap, and the comparison.
- Define the median, box, whiskers, and outlying points. Never infer SD, SEM, CI, or whisker rules from appearance alone.
- There is no universal sample-size cutoff for dot plots. Small-sample violin contours should not be treated as reliable evidence of distribution shape.
- Lines require a meaningful ordering and connection. Cross-sectional age differences are not within-person trajectories; nonlongitudinal data are not categorically forbidden from line plots.
- Use comparable scales for directly compared panels where appropriate; clearly indicate necessary scale differences.
- Reduce excessive grouping, 3D decoration, and unnecessary grids. Use labels, shape, and accessible colors; consider grayscale readability.
- Organize tables around the intended comparison, preserving key estimates and uncertainty.

## Legend architecture

Descriptive title → panels and subjects/conditions → symbols, units, sample sizes, and error definitions → supported statistical details and abbreviations.

Identify what each panel shows, the groups and comparator, what n represents, biological versus technical replication when relevant, and definitions of dots, lines, colors, boxes, whiskers, error bars, abbreviations, and scale bars. Preserve figure numbers, panel labels, units, and numerical precision.

Ask for undefined error bars or replication units; do not silently write `mean ± SEM` or `independent biological replicates`. Flag contradictions between text and figure. Statistical methods and multiplicity statements must come from the actual study.

## Images

Retain original acquisitions and metadata where available; work on copies and document processing. Polishing does not authorize cloning, erasing, selectively enhancing evidence, or generating replacement experimental images. Visual review can assess labeling, scale, readability, and disclosed processing, but cannot certify data authenticity.
