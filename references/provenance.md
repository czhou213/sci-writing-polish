# Scope and Attribution

## Self-contained use

The bundled references contain the operational guidance needed for editing. They do not require access to a note vault, private course material, a particular filesystem, or an external service. Examples are fictional editing exercises; bracketed frames describe optional functions and must be filled only with supplied evidence.

## Acknowledgment and boundaries

The skill's development was informed in part by Nature Masterclasses teaching on research communication. The guidance here is an editorial synthesis, not an official course, a reproduction of its teaching materials, or an endorsed Nature product. It does not claim that every recommendation or example comes from that training.

Named ideas such as topic position, stress position, and signposting are used as writing concepts. Functional sequences are adaptable editing aids, not quotations, fixed templates, or journal requirements. Do not claim that private slides or transcripts were checked during an editing task.

## Applicability

Use the article's actual discipline, study design, audience, and requested editing depth. Do not enforce sentence counts, voice percentages, title lengths, or chart sample-size cutoffs without an applicable requirement. Improve clarity without promising publication, citations, search ranking, or a particular AI-detector outcome.

Ordinary editing preserves author-supplied science; it does not verify the cited literature, validate analyses, or establish compliance with current journal policy. When the user requests those checks, consult the relevant available sources and distinguish verified information from supplied assertions.
