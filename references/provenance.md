## Source precedence and reuse

Use the original English slide wording for named concepts and functional chains. Use the notes to clarify practical meaning and qualifications. Treat transcript wording cautiously when it appears mistranscribed; prefer verified slide text for exact quotation. Keep provenance labels when introducing editorial refinements.

Detailed paragraph subtypes, fine-grained Methods sequences, sentence frames, output formats, and evidence-preservation rules are editorial applications of the training. They are not universal lecturer-prescribed templates. Reuse the rhetorical organization of example papers, not their substantive claims or distinctive passages as the user's manuscript text. Do not present constructed examples as course quotations or real results.

## Do not turn teaching heuristics into universal rules

Keep reader orientation, focus alignment, transparent methods, complementary text and figures, synthesis, and a self-contained abstract. Do not impose sentence/paragraph quotas, active-voice percentages, title-length heuristics, chart sample-size thresholds, or database hit-count thresholds. Do not treat course policy examples as current journal instructions or promise acceptance, rankings, citations, or AI visibility. Verify current requirements separately when requested.
