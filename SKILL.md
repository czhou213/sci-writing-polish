# SCI Writing: Language, Structure, and Reasoning

Help readers understand why the study was needed, what was done, what was found, and why it matters. Use the Day 1 Nature Masterclasses framework: **Be concise. Be specific. Be logical.** These are writing principles, not journal acceptance criteria.

## English-first and source-faithful

Write manuscript revisions, proposed sentences, paragraph plans, and writing examples in English by default. When the user supplies Chinese research notes for an English paper, develop English prose from those notes without inventing missing information. Follow an explicitly requested output language. Explanations may follow the user's conversation language; keep technical writing labels in English when useful.

Prefer the original English teaching terminology and functional sequences quoted in the references over back-translations of Chinese notes. Read [provenance.md](references/provenance.md) when checking attribution. Labels marked **Course wording** are verified slide excerpts; **Editorial application** identifies operational extensions and constructed sentence frames. Adapt rhetorical functions to the manuscript; do not import scientific claims, data, or long passages from the course's example papers.

## Scope and depth

Infer the section, audience, task, and depth from the supplied material. Proceed when enough information is available.

- **Light:** Grammar, spelling, and minimal wording changes; preserve information order when requested.
- **Standard (default):** Improve both language and content/reasoning. Identify each paragraph's purpose, reorganize existing material within it, and refine sentence functions and transitions. Propose cross-paragraph moves separately.
- **Deep:** When restructuring is requested, reorganize paragraphs and the argument; explain substantive moves and retain counterevidence, null findings, and limitations.
- **Draft from supplied material:** When asked to write, map supplied findings, methods, and cited evidence to the appropriate paragraph functions. Draft supported material in English; list missing evidence separately. Do not treat empty functional slots as permission to invent a complete study.

A single paragraph does not require a full manuscript report. Without a named journal, use field-appropriate scientific English. Interpret “Nature style” as reader-oriented clarity. Resolve language issues directly; ask only about ambiguity that affects scientific meaning, and continue with unaffected material.

## Two editing dimensions, three levels

**Language:** Grammar, precise vocabulary and referents, subject–verb proximity, topic and stress positions, signposting, voice, strong verbs, concision, and rhythm.

**Content and reasoning:** Paragraph purpose, relevance and completeness of evidence, separation of observation from interpretation, information order, and the significance of the paragraph for the reader.

Work from **section purpose → paragraph role and main message → sentence functions**. Identify where sentences should be retained, moved, merged, split, or clarified. Replace vague advice such as “improve the logic” with an actual revision or a specific evidence request. Reorder supplied information; clarify supported relationships; flag claims requiring new evidence or author judgment.

## Scientific meaning and evidence

Before editing, identify the applicable invariants: design; population and sample size; intervention/exposure/comparator; outcomes and time points; effect direction, numbers, units, and uncertainty; citations and figure labels; limitations and claim strength.

- Preserve these facts and their qualifiers. Do not change analysis populations, comparators, primary/secondary endpoints, or follow-up through stylistic edits.
- Do not invent experiments, effects, methods, ethics approval, registration, references, or mechanisms. Flag inconsistent numbers rather than silently repairing them.
- Do not convert association into causation, exploratory results into confirmation, lack of detected difference into equivalence, or statistical significance into clinical benefit.
- Retain important negative findings and limitations. Do not strengthen a conclusion merely to make it persuasive or hedge every established fact mechanically.
- If the original overclaims relative to its stated evidence, identify that problem and offer a calibrated revision; label this as a substantive correction, not just language polishing.
- Do not endorse unsupported “first,” “novel,” “superior,” or “safe and effective” claims. Keep citations attached to the claims they support; citation presence does not establish source verification.

Ordinary editing uses the supplied material. When external fact-checking, new references, or current journal requirements are requested, verify appropriate sources and distinguish externally checked facts from author-supplied information.

## Edit and verify

1. Identify the audience, focus, and research question. Track the intervention/variable/condition, outcome, and measurement source where applicable.
2. Resolve paragraph roles and sentence order before polishing vocabulary. In deep editing, align research question, aim, evidence, and conclusion.
3. Compare the revision with the original for numbers, units, directions, citations, negation, uncertainty, exceptions, and scope. Check that compression has not removed reproducibility details or contrary evidence.
4. Deliver supported revisions; isolate unresolved scientific ambiguities and missing evidence.

Retain already effective prose. Do not impose fixed sentence counts, a 10–20-word limit, voice quotas, or indiscriminate synonym replacement.

## Deliverables

Lead with usable English text, followed by concise **Content and reasoning** and **Language** notes, and author queries only where needed. Follow requests for revised text only. Do not manufacture changes in an already satisfactory dimension.

For a paragraph, include a short functional sequence when restructuring matters. For a section, use stable P1/P2 identifiers and a compact table for substantive changes: paragraph purpose, proposed sentence-function sequence, content changes, and language changes. Cover every paragraph only when detailed review is requested. Identify whether S1/S2 labels refer to original or revised text; omit teaching labels from the finished manuscript.

For titles, offer the requested number or one recommendation with genuinely different alternatives. For word limits, count the final text and state the counting convention. Do not claim compliance with an unknown journal counter.

Respect file type, revision-tracking, and destination requests. Preserve a recoverable original. Do not claim native Track Changes without creating them. Report the actual review scope: language editing is not statistical validation, and unseen figures cannot be visually verified.
